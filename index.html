1.2 · How I read it

Break every cost into the step that caused it

★
Bottom line
A cost per trial is a product of three numbers. Find the weak one and you know what to change: the hook, the click, or who's clicking.
Cost per trial = CPM ÷ 1,000 ÷ (CTR × click-to-trial rate)

CPM is what the auction charges. CTR shows whether the ad stops the scroll and earns the click. Click-to-trial rate shows whether the people who click actually want the product. For videos, hook and hold rate come before CTR.

Chapter 4

The idea

What do I say to her, and how does Jyota sound saying it?
4.1 · The insight
One true thing most wellness brands miss
She doesn't fall off the plan. Her week moves, most plans don't, and she's the one who ends up feeling like she failed.

What the category gets wrong

Most brands treat her as the thing to fix: more motivation, more discipline, a 28-day challenge. But a woman in her 40s or 50s isn't short on discipline. She runs a job, a household and often a parent's care. What breaks is the plan, which assumes every week looks the same.

Where it comes from

  • The data: the winners (B, F) promise a plan that fits her life. The fitness-led ads lost.
  • A's line: “every app was made for 25-year-olds” had the second-best hook. Blaming the plan, not her, stops the scroll.
  • The market: Noom and Midi both take the blame off her (H6).
  • The brief: “A plan that keeps up with her life, not the other way around.”

4.7 · Meta policy check

Two things I stay clear of

1 · Personal attributes

The risk: lines that assert or imply something about the viewer, such as “Over 40?”, “Struggling with menopause?” or “your changing body”. Wellness ads get rejected for this all the time.

How I stay clear: age appears only in a character's own first-person line (“I'm 51”). Every hook is about her week, never her body or health. Competitors run “Ladies, over 35…”, but Jyota doesn't need to.

2 · Before/after and body outcomes

The risk: before/after images, body-part close-ups, weight numbers or implied health results. All are restricted, and all break the brief's honesty rule.

How I stay clear: the only before and after I ever show is a plan: Tuesday's plan, then the rebuilt one. No body close-ups, no weight numbers, no promises about results.

★
Bonus · honest testimony

No AI character claims a result from Jyota, and each one is labeled: “Dramatization” in the video, “AI-generated image” on the static. Real testimony waits for real users, posted as labeled creator partnerships, the way Pvolve and Midi do it.

6.1 · The decision rules

Four questions, one weak link, one change

Chapter 1 sorted the 8 ads by judgment. This turns that judgment into rules I can run on every ad, every week. Cost per trial breaks into a chain of links. Each link belongs to one part of the ad, and I fix only the first link that leaks.

Cost per trial = CPM ÷ 1,000 ÷ (CTR × click-to-trial)

Video CTR = hook × hold × clicks per ThruPlay

Each link is read against the account: 1.0× is the average of the 8 ads, 0.8× or less is weak, 1.2× or more is strong. For CPM, cheaper counts as stronger. In video, CTR isn't its own link: it's already the hook, the hold and the clicks multiplied together.

What each link means, and what I change when it's weak

Run the rules

Filled in with a made-up week for my Plans Change video: a strong hook and a weak hold, the case in the brief's question 6. Change any number.

    All 8 ads through the rules

    ▲ strong (1.2× or more) · ▼ weak (0.8× or less) · ● the link the rules fix · – not in that format's chain

    ?
    Assumption

    Run on the 8 ads, the rules reach the same call as chapter 1 for every one, but the 0.8× and 1.2× lines were chosen on the same 8 ads. The next batch is the real test. They also point at the same part to fix: C's opening, A's body, D's match with the page, E's thumb-stop.

    ?
    Assumption

    The 0.8× and 1.2× lines are my call, not a standard, and the account is only 8 ads. As spend grows, the baseline should move to a rolling 4 weeks, and the lines should be checked against what happened to the ads they flagged.

    ?
    Assumption

    H is a close call. Its clicks per ThruPlay is 1.16×, just under strong. At a 1.15× line the rules would say “fix the CPM”, which isn't a creative fix. Close calls go to a person, not the rule.

    ✕
    Set aside

    CPC as its own rule. CPC = CPM ÷ CTR, so it's already in the chain. “High CPC, new hook” is right only when the CTR is the cause. When the CPM is, a new hook changes nothing.

    ✕
    Set aside

    Fatigue. There's no frequency data yet. Once there is, a falling hook rate at a steady CPM is the first sign, and the fix is a new first second, not a new ad.

    ★
    Bottom line
    Every ad, every week, gets the same four questions. The answer is rarely “make a new ad”. It's “change this one part”.
    One change per variation keeps every test readable, and it's how the next 20 ads take half the time.

    Let Meta scale the winner. Keep the test a fair race.

    Why pairs

    Each new ad runs against its original in one Meta A/B test: same audience, same days, an even split. A lift can't come from a different week or a different delivery.

    Why these metrics

    Each test is read on the step it changes. Hook rate and CTR have thousands of events in two weeks; trials have dozens. A ~10% lift in hook rate shows, a 20% gap in cost per trial doesn't.

    Day 14

    ?
    Assumption
